# Malta Year 7 Math — Year 7 (Short) Unit 1 to Unit 3

A short pass through the first three Year 7 units — number system, numerical calculations and algebra — with one idea and one worked example per subtopic.

## Unit 1 — Number System

### 1. Place value and ordering

A digit's value depends on its column. To order numbers, compare the biggest place value first; the first column that differs decides.

**Example:** Order 40,312, 40,231 and 43,012 from smallest to largest.

<details>
<summary><b>Working</b></summary>

All three have 4 ten-thousands. In the thousands column, 43,012 has 3 and the others have 0, so it is the largest.

Compare the hundreds of the other two: 2 < 3.

$$
\boxed{40{,}231 < 40{,}312 < 43{,}012}
$$

</details>

### 2. Factors, multiples, primes and LCM

A **factor** divides a number exactly. A **multiple** is the number times 1, 2, 3, … A **prime** has exactly two factors, 1 and itself. The **LCM** is the smallest multiple two numbers share.

**Example:** List the factors of 18, say which are prime, and find the LCM of 4 and 10.

<details>
<summary><b>Working</b></summary>

Pair the factors so none are missed:

$$
18 = 1\times18 = 2\times9 = 3\times6
$$

Factors: 1, 2, 3, 6, 9, 18. The primes among them are 2 and 3.

Multiples of 10 are 10, 20, … and 20 is the first one that 4 divides:

$$
\text{LCM}(4, 10) = \boxed{20}
$$

</details>

### 3. Squares and cubes

A **square** multiplies a number by itself, $n^2 = n\times n$. A **cube** multiplies three copies, $n^3 = n\times n\times n$.

**Example:** Find $7^2$ and $4^3$. Which is bigger?

<details>
<summary><b>Working</b></summary>

$$
7^2 = 7\times7 = 49
$$

$$
4^3 = 4\times4\times4 = 64
$$

So $\boxed{4^3 = 64}$ is bigger, even though 4 is smaller than 7.

</details>

### 4. Decimals, percentages and negative numbers

Percent means out of 100, so $0.35 = \frac{35}{100} = 35\%$. On a number line, numbers further right are bigger, so $-2 > -6$.

**Example:** Write 0.6 as a percentage, then order $-4$, 0.6, $-1.5$ and 0 from smallest to largest.

<details>
<summary><b>Working</b></summary>

Tenths become hundredths:

$$
0.6 = \frac{60}{100} = 60\%
$$

Not 6% — that would be 0.06.

The negative number furthest from 0 is the smallest:

$$
\boxed{-4 < -1.5 < 0 < 0.6}
$$

</details>

## Unit 2 — Numerical Calculations

### 5. Arithmetic and BIDMAS

Work in this order: **B**rackets, **I**ndices, **D**ivision and **M**ultiplication (left to right), **A**ddition and **S**ubtraction (left to right).

**Example:** Calculate $20 - 3\times(2 + 4)\div9$.

<details>
<summary><b>Working</b></summary>

Brackets first:

$$
20 - 3\times6\div9
$$

× and ÷ from left to right, then the subtraction:

$$
20 - 18\div9 = 20 - 2 = \boxed{18}
$$

</details>

### 6. Rounding

Find the digit in the place you round to and look at the next digit: 5 or more rounds up, 4 or less leaves it.

**Example:** Round 7.2851 to 1 decimal place and to 2 decimal places.

<details>
<summary><b>Working</b></summary>

1 decimal place: the next digit after 7.2 is 8, so round up:

$$
7.2851 \approx \boxed{7.3}
$$

2 decimal places: the next digit after 7.28 is 5, so round up:

$$
7.2851 \approx \boxed{7.29}
$$

</details>

### 7. Fraction arithmetic

Add or subtract with a common denominator. Multiply tops and bottoms. To divide, multiply by the reciprocal.

**Example:** Calculate $\frac56 - \frac38$ and $\frac25 \div \frac{4}{15}$.

<details>
<summary><b>Working</b></summary>

The common denominator of 6 and 8 is 24:

$$
\frac56 - \frac38 = \frac{20}{24} - \frac{9}{24} = \boxed{\frac{11}{24}}
$$

Flip the second fraction and multiply:

$$
\frac25\times\frac{15}{4} = \frac{30}{20} = \boxed{\frac32}
$$

</details>

### 8. Percentages of quantities

$p\%$ of an amount is $\frac{p}{100}$ of it. 10% is a tenth, so other percentages can be built from 10%, 5% and 1%.

**Example:** Find 45% of 240.

<details>
<summary><b>Working</b></summary>

10% of 240 is 24, so

$$
40\% = 96, \qquad 5\% = 12
$$

Add, and check with the decimal:

$$
45\% = 96 + 12 = 0.45\times240 = \boxed{108}
$$

</details>

### 9. Money and direct proportion

When every item costs the same, find the cost of one item first, then scale up. Keep money to two decimal places.

**Example:** 6 ice creams cost €9.00. How much do 10 cost?

<details>
<summary><b>Working</b></summary>

One ice cream:

$$
9.00\div6 = 1.50
$$

Ten ice creams:

$$
10\times1.50 = \boxed{15.00}
$$

Ten cost €15.00.

</details>

## Unit 3 — Algebra

### 10. Sequences

In a linear sequence the terms change by the same step. A step of $d$ gives $dn$ in the rule for the nth term; then add or subtract to match the first term.

**Example:** Find the next term and the nth term of 7, 11, 15, 19, …

<details>
<summary><b>Working</b></summary>

The step is 4, so the next term is $19 + 4 = 23$.

The rule starts with $4n$. When $n = 1$, $4n = 4$, but the first term is 7, so add 3:

$$
\boxed{4n + 3}
$$

</details>

### 11. Expressions and brackets

**Like terms** have the same letter part and can be collected. To expand a bracket, multiply every term inside by the term outside.

**Example:** Simplify $4(2a - 3) + 5a + 2$.

<details>
<summary><b>Working</b></summary>

Expand:

$$
8a - 12 + 5a + 2
$$

Collect the $a$ terms and the numbers:

$$
\boxed{13a - 10}
$$

</details>

### 12. Substitution

Replace each letter with its value in brackets, then follow BIDMAS.

**Example:** Find $2a^2 - 3b$ when $a = 3$ and $b = -4$.

<details>
<summary><b>Working</b></summary>

Substitute, with brackets:

$$
2(3)^2 - 3(-4)
$$

Square first, then multiply; subtracting $-12$ adds 12:

$$
2\times9 + 12 = \boxed{30}
$$

</details>

### 13. Simple equations

Do the same to both sides, undoing the operations in reverse order.

**Example:** Solve $5x - 7 = 18$.

<details>
<summary><b>Working</b></summary>

Add 7 to both sides:

$$
5x = 25
$$

Divide both sides by 5:

$$
\boxed{x = 5}
$$

Check: $5\times5 - 7 = 18$.

</details>

### 14. Coordinates, linear graphs and function machines

A point $(x, y)$ is across first, then up. A function machine turns each input $x$ into an output $y$; plotting the pairs of a rule such as $y = 3x - 1$ gives a straight line.

**Example:** A machine multiplies by 3, then subtracts 1. Find the outputs for inputs 0, 1 and 2, and the rule.

<details>
<summary><b>Working</b></summary>

Run each input through the machine:

$$
\begin{aligned} 0&\to 3(0) - 1 = -1 \\ 1&\to 3(1) - 1 = 2 \\ 2&\to 3(2) - 1 = 5 \end{aligned}
$$

The points $(0, -1)$, $(1, 2)$ and $(2, 5)$ lie on one straight line:

$$
\boxed{y = 3x - 1}
$$

</details>
