# Malta Year 8 Math Chapter 2 — Number Work

Chapter 2 builds the number skills that every later chapter uses: place value and rounding (2.1), the order of operations, BIDMAS (2.2), factors, multiples and primes (2.3), the HCF and the LCM (2.4), and squares, cubes and roots (2.5). Labels 2.1 to 2.5 name the parts of this page. Every card has several examples of the same kind — try each one, then tap **Show working**.

**By the end of the chapter:** round numbers, work out calculations in the right order, find factors, primes, the HCF and the LCM, and use squares, cubes and their roots.

## 2.1 Place Value and Rounding

### 1. Value of a digit

Each place is worth **10 times** the place on its right: … thousands, hundreds, tens, units **.** tenths, hundredths. The value of a digit depends on its place.

**Examples:**

- **a)** the 7 in $4\,725$
- **b)** the 7 in $73\,018$
- **c)** the 7 in $12.7$
- **d)** the 7 in $3.07$

<details>
<summary><b>Working</b></summary>

**a)** The 7 is in the hundreds place:

$$
\boxed{700}
$$

**b)** The 7 is in the ten-thousands place:

$$
\boxed{70\,000}
$$

**c)** The 7 is in the tenths place:

$$
\boxed{0.7}
$$

**d)** The 7 is in the hundredths place:

$$
\boxed{0.07}
$$

</details>

### 2. Rounding whole numbers

Look at the digit **just after** the place you are rounding to: **5 or more** rounds **up**, **4 or less** rounds **down**.

**Examples:**

- **a)** $347$ to the nearest 10
- **b)** $2\,461$ to the nearest 100
- **c)** $18\,499$ to the nearest 1000
- **d)** $795$ to the nearest 10

<details>
<summary><b>Working</b></summary>

**a)** Next digit is 7, so round up:

$$
347 \approx \boxed{350}
$$

**b)** Next digit is 6, so round up:

$$
2\,461 \approx \boxed{2\,500}
$$

**c)** Next digit is 4, so round down:

$$
18\,499 \approx \boxed{18\,000}
$$

**d)** Next digit is 5, so round up — 79 tens become 80 tens:

$$
795 \approx \boxed{800}
$$

</details>

### 3. Rounding decimals

The same rule works after the decimal point: count the decimal places, then look at the **next** digit.

**Examples:**

- **a)** $3.46$ to 1 decimal place
- **b)** $12.83$ to the nearest whole number
- **c)** $0.372$ to 2 decimal places
- **d)** $9.96$ to 1 decimal place

<details>
<summary><b>Working</b></summary>

**a)** Next digit is 6, so round up:

$$
3.46 \approx \boxed{3.5}
$$

**b)** Next digit is 8, so round up:

$$
12.83 \approx \boxed{13}
$$

**c)** Next digit is 2, so round down:

$$
0.372 \approx \boxed{0.37}
$$

**d)** Next digit is 6, so round up — 9.9 becomes 10.0:

$$
9.96 \approx \boxed{10.0}
$$

</details>

## 2.2 BIDMAS

### 4. × and ÷ before + and −

**BIDMAS** gives the order: **B**rackets, **I**ndices (powers), **D**ivision and **M**ultiplication, then **A**ddition and **S**ubtraction. So multiply or divide **before** you add or subtract.

**Examples:**

- **a)** $5 + 3\times4$
- **b)** $20 - 12\div4$
- **c)** $6\times2 + 8\div2$
- **d)** $30 - 4\times5 + 1$

<details>
<summary><b>Working</b></summary>

**a)**

$$
5 + 3\times4 = 5 + 12 = \boxed{17}
$$

**b)**

$$
20 - 12\div4 = 20 - 3 = \boxed{17}
$$

**c)**

$$
6\times2 + 8\div2 = 12 + 4 = \boxed{16}
$$

**d)**

$$
30 - 4\times5 + 1 = 30 - 20 + 1 = \boxed{11}
$$

</details>

### 5. Brackets first

Work out the **brackets first** — they change the order.

**Examples:**

- **a)** $(5 + 3)\times4$
- **b)** $20\div(2 + 3)$
- **c)** $3\times(10 - 4)$
- **d)** $(8 - 2)\times(1 + 4)$

<details>
<summary><b>Working</b></summary>

**a)**

$$
(5 + 3)\times4 = 8\times4 = \boxed{32}
$$

**b)**

$$
20\div(2 + 3) = 20\div5 = \boxed{4}
$$

**c)**

$$
3\times(10 - 4) = 3\times6 = \boxed{18}
$$

**d)**

$$
(8 - 2)\times(1 + 4) = 6\times5 = \boxed{30}
$$

</details>

### 6. Powers before × and ÷

**Indices** (powers such as $3^2$) come straight after brackets.

**Examples:**

- **a)** $2\times3^2$
- **b)** $4^2 - 6$
- **c)** $(1 + 2)^2$
- **d)** $10 + 2^3\div4$

<details>
<summary><b>Working</b></summary>

**a)**

$$
2\times3^2 = 2\times9 = \boxed{18}
$$

**b)**

$$
4^2 - 6 = 16 - 6 = \boxed{10}
$$

**c)** Bracket first, then the power:

$$
(1 + 2)^2 = 3^2 = \boxed{9}
$$

**d)**

$$
10 + 2^3\div4 = 10 + 8\div4 = 10 + 2 = \boxed{12}
$$

</details>

## 2.3 Factors, Multiples and Primes

### 7. Factors

A **factor** divides a number exactly, with no remainder. Find factors in **pairs** that multiply to the number, and stop when the pairs meet.

**Examples:**

- **a)** the factors of 18
- **b)** the factors of 20
- **c)** the factors of 36
- **d)** the factors of 13

<details>
<summary><b>Working</b></summary>

**a)** Pairs $1\times18,\ 2\times9,\ 3\times6$:

$$
\boxed{1,\ 2,\ 3,\ 6,\ 9,\ 18}
$$

**b)** Pairs $1\times20,\ 2\times10,\ 4\times5$:

$$
\boxed{1,\ 2,\ 4,\ 5,\ 10,\ 20}
$$

**c)** Pairs $1\times36,\ 2\times18,\ 3\times12,\ 4\times9,\ 6\times6$:

$$
\boxed{1,\ 2,\ 3,\ 4,\ 6,\ 9,\ 12,\ 18,\ 36}
$$

**d)** Pairs $1\times13$:

$$
\boxed{1,\ 13}
$$

13 has only two factors — it is a prime number.

</details>

### 8. Multiples

The **multiples** of a number are its times table: the multiples of 4 are 4, 8, 12, 16, … A number is a multiple of 7 when 7 divides it exactly.

**Examples:**

- **a)** the first five multiples of 6
- **b)** the first five multiples of 9
- **c)** Is 84 a multiple of 7?
- **d)** Is 50 a multiple of 4?

<details>
<summary><b>Working</b></summary>

**a)**

$$
\boxed{6,\ 12,\ 18,\ 24,\ 30}
$$

**b)**

$$
\boxed{9,\ 18,\ 27,\ 36,\ 45}
$$

**c)** $84\div7 = 12$ exactly:

$$
\boxed{\text{Yes: } 84 = 7\times12}
$$

**d)** $50\div4 = 12.5$, not a whole number:

$$
\boxed{\text{No}}
$$

</details>

### 9. Prime numbers

A **prime number** has **exactly two factors**: 1 and itself. The primes below 20 are $2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17, 19$. The number **1 is not prime** — it has only one factor.

**Examples:**

- **a)** Is 17 prime?
- **b)** Is 21 prime?
- **c)** Is 29 prime?
- **d)** Is 1 prime?

<details>
<summary><b>Working</b></summary>

**a)** Only $1\times17$:

$$
\boxed{17 \text{ is prime}}
$$

**b)** $21 = 3\times7$, so it has more than two factors:

$$
\boxed{21 \text{ is not prime}}
$$

**c)** 2, 3 and 5 do not divide 29, so only $1\times29$:

$$
\boxed{29 \text{ is prime}}
$$

**d)** 1 has only one factor:

$$
\boxed{1 \text{ is not prime}}
$$

</details>

### 10. Prime factor trees

Split the number into two factors, and keep splitting until every branch ends in a **prime**. Then write the number as a product of primes, using powers for repeats: $2\times2\times2 = 2^3$.

**Examples:**

- **a)** $12$
- **b)** $30$
- **c)** $40$
- **d)** $45$

<details>
<summary><b>Working</b></summary>

**a)**

$$
12 = 2\times6 = 2\times2\times3 = \boxed{2^2\times3}
$$

**b)**

$$
30 = 2\times15 = \boxed{2\times3\times5}
$$

**c)**

$$
40 = 2\times20 = 2\times2\times10 = \boxed{2^3\times5}
$$

**d)**

$$
45 = 5\times9 = \boxed{3^2\times5}
$$

</details>

## 2.4 HCF and LCM

### 11. Highest common factor (HCF)

The **HCF** is the **biggest** number that divides **both** numbers exactly. List the factors of each, then pick the biggest one that is in both lists.

**Examples:**

- **a)** HCF of 12 and 18
- **b)** HCF of 8 and 20
- **c)** HCF of 15 and 25
- **d)** HCF of 14 and 21

<details>
<summary><b>Working</b></summary>

**a)** Factors of 12: 1, 2, 3, 4, **6**, 12. Factors of 18: 1, 2, 3, **6**, 9, 18.

$$
\text{HCF} = \boxed{6}
$$

**b)** Factors of 8: 1, 2, **4**, 8. Factors of 20: 1, 2, **4**, 5, 10, 20.

$$
\text{HCF} = \boxed{4}
$$

**c)** Factors of 15: 1, 3, **5**, 15. Factors of 25: 1, **5**, 25.

$$
\text{HCF} = \boxed{5}
$$

**d)** Factors of 14: 1, 2, **7**, 14. Factors of 21: 1, 3, **7**, 21.

$$
\text{HCF} = \boxed{7}
$$

</details>

### 12. Lowest common multiple (LCM)

The **LCM** is the **smallest** number that is in **both** times tables. List the multiples of each number until one appears in both lists.

**Examples:**

- **a)** LCM of 4 and 6
- **b)** LCM of 3 and 5
- **c)** LCM of 6 and 8
- **d)** LCM of 10 and 15

<details>
<summary><b>Working</b></summary>

**a)** Multiples of 4: 4, 8, **12**. Multiples of 6: 6, **12**.

$$
\text{LCM} = \boxed{12}
$$

**b)** Multiples of 3: 3, 6, 9, 12, **15**. Multiples of 5: 5, 10, **15**.

$$
\text{LCM} = \boxed{15}
$$

**c)** Multiples of 6: 6, 12, 18, **24**. Multiples of 8: 8, 16, **24**.

$$
\text{LCM} = \boxed{24}
$$

**d)** Multiples of 10: 10, 20, **30**. Multiples of 15: 15, **30**.

$$
\text{LCM} = \boxed{30}
$$

</details>

### 13. HCF or LCM?

**LCM** answers *when do they happen together again?* **HCF** answers *what is the biggest equal group or piece?*

**Examples:**

- **a)** Two buses leave Valletta together at 9:00. One route leaves every 10 minutes, the other every 15 minutes. When do they next leave together?
- **b)** 24 pastizzi and 36 qassatat are packed into bags. Every bag holds the same number of pastries, one kind per bag, with none left over. What is the most each bag can hold?
- **c)** Ribbons of 18 cm and 27 cm are cut into pieces all the same length, with nothing left over. What is the longest each piece can be?
- **d)** One lighthouse flashes every 6 seconds and another every 8 seconds. They flash together now. After how many seconds do they flash together again?

<details>
<summary><b>Working</b></summary>

**a)** Happen together again → LCM of 10 and 15 is 30 minutes:

$$
\boxed{9{:}30}
$$

**b)** Biggest equal group → HCF of 24 and 36:

$$
\boxed{12 \text{ per bag}}
$$

That makes 2 bags of pastizzi and 3 bags of qassatat.

**c)** Biggest equal piece → HCF of 18 and 27:

$$
\boxed{9 \text{ cm}}
$$

**d)** Happen together again → LCM of 6 and 8:

$$
\boxed{24 \text{ seconds}}
$$

</details>

## 2.5 Squares, Cubes and Roots

### 14. Square numbers

A **square number** is a number times itself: $n^2 = n\times n$. The first ten are 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49, 64, 81, 100.

**Examples:**

- **a)** $6^2$
- **b)** $9^2$
- **c)** $11^2$
- **d)** $15^2$

<details>
<summary><b>Working</b></summary>

**a)**

$$
6^2 = 6\times6 = \boxed{36}
$$

Not $6\times2 = 12$.

**b)**

$$
9^2 = 9\times9 = \boxed{81}
$$

**c)**

$$
11^2 = 11\times11 = \boxed{121}
$$

**d)**

$$
15^2 = 15\times15 = \boxed{225}
$$

</details>

### 15. Square roots

The **square root** $\sqrt{\ }$ undoes squaring: $\sqrt{25} = 5$ because $5^2 = 25$. Ask: *which number times itself makes this?*

**Examples:**

- **a)** $\sqrt{64}$
- **b)** $\sqrt{100}$
- **c)** $\sqrt{144}$
- **d)** $\sqrt{49}$

<details>
<summary><b>Working</b></summary>

**a)** $8\times8 = 64$:

$$
\sqrt{64} = \boxed{8}
$$

**b)** $10\times10 = 100$:

$$
\sqrt{100} = \boxed{10}
$$

**c)** $12\times12 = 144$:

$$
\sqrt{144} = \boxed{12}
$$

**d)** $7\times7 = 49$:

$$
\sqrt{49} = \boxed{7}
$$

</details>

### 16. Cubes and cube roots

A **cube** uses a number three times: $n^3 = n\times n\times n$. The **cube root** $\sqrt[3]{\ }$ undoes it: $\sqrt[3]{8} = 2$.

**Examples:**

- **a)** $2^3$
- **b)** $4^3$
- **c)** $\sqrt[3]{27}$
- **d)** $\sqrt[3]{125}$

<details>
<summary><b>Working</b></summary>

**a)**

$$
2^3 = 2\times2\times2 = \boxed{8}
$$

**b)**

$$
4^3 = 4\times4\times4 = \boxed{64}
$$

**c)** $3\times3\times3 = 27$:

$$
\sqrt[3]{27} = \boxed{3}
$$

**d)** $5\times5\times5 = 125$:

$$
\sqrt[3]{125} = \boxed{5}
$$

</details>

### 17. Powers

In $3^4$ the **base** 3 is multiplied by itself, and the **power** 4 says how many threes: $3^4 = 3\times3\times3\times3$.

**Examples:**

- **a)** $3^4$
- **b)** $10^3$
- **c)** $2^5$
- **d)** $5^2\times2$

<details>
<summary><b>Working</b></summary>

**a)**

$$
3^4 = 3\times3\times3\times3 = \boxed{81}
$$

**b)**

$$
10^3 = 10\times10\times10 = \boxed{1000}
$$

**c)**

$$
2^5 = 2\times2\times2\times2\times2 = \boxed{32}
$$

**d)** Power first:

$$
5^2\times2 = 25\times2 = \boxed{50}
$$

</details>
