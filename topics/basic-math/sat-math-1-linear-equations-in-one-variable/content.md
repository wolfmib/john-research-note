# SAT Math 1 — Linear Equations in One Variable

**SAT domain:** Algebra (13–15 of the 44 math questions).

**Key ideas**
- Do the same thing to both sides until $x$ is alone.
- Clear fractions first: multiply every term by the lowest common denominator (LCD).
- Clear decimals the same way: multiply by 10 or 100.
- After simplifying to $ax + b = cx + d$:
  - $a \ne c$: exactly one solution.
  - $a = c$ and $b \ne d$: no solution.
  - $a = c$ and $b = d$: infinitely many solutions.
- The SAT often asks for an expression such as $3x - 1$, not $x$. Look for a shortcut that gets it straight from the equation.

**Watch out**
- Distribute a minus sign to every term in the bracket: $-(x - 4) = -x + 4$.
- Multiply **every** term by the LCD, including whole numbers.

## 1. Exam 1 — Fractions on both sides

What value of $x$ satisfies the equation?

$$
\frac{2}{3}(x - 6) + 5 = \frac{x}{2}
$$

**A.** $-6$

**B.** $6$

**C.** $-18$

**D.** $18$

<details>
<summary><b>Working</b></summary>

Multiply every term by the LCD, 6:

$$
4(x - 6) + 30 = 3x
$$

$$
\begin{aligned} 4x - 24 + 30 &= 3x \\ 4x + 6 &= 3x \\ x &= -6 \end{aligned}
$$

Check both sides at $x = -6$:

$$
\begin{aligned} \tfrac{2}{3}(-12) + 5 &= -3 \\ \tfrac{-6}{2} &= -3 \end{aligned}
$$

$$
\boxed{x = -6}
$$

**Answer: A**

</details>

## 2. Exam 2 — Infinitely many solutions

In the equation below, $k$ is a constant. The equation has infinitely many solutions.

$$
5(2x - k) + 3 = 10x - 12
$$

What is the value of $k$? (Type the answer.)

<details>
<summary><b>Working</b></summary>

Expand the left side:

$$
10x - 5k + 3 = 10x - 12
$$

The $x$-terms already match. Infinitely many solutions needs the constants to match too:

$$
\begin{aligned} -5k + 3 &= -12 \\ -5k &= -15 \\ k &= 3 \end{aligned}
$$

Any other $k$ leaves $10x + \text{(one number)} = 10x + \text{(another number)}$: no solution.

$$
\boxed{k = 3}
$$

</details>
