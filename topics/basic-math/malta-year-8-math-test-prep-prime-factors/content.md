# Malta Year 8 Math — Prime Factors

- A **prime number** has exactly two positive factors: 1 and itself.
- 1 is not prime; 2 is the only even prime.
- Prime factorisation writes a whole number as a product of primes.
- Powers show how many times a prime is repeated.
- Extension: use prime factors to find HCF and LCM.

## 1. Style 1 · Break a number into primes

**How it works**

- Divide by the smallest prime possible: 2, then 3, then 5, and so on.
- Continue until the remaining number is prime. Every final factor must be prime.
- Combine repeated factors using powers.

**Worked example:** factorise **84 and 126**, use index notation, and identify their common prime factors.

- **Your turn:** Factorise **60 and 90** on paper and write each using powers.
- Identify the common prime factors.
- Enter each exponent below; an unwritten exponent is 1.

| Question | Answer |
|---:|---:|
| $60:\ \text{power of }2$ | ? |
| $60:\ \text{power of }3$ | ? |
| $60:\ \text{power of }5$ | ? |
| $90:\ \text{power of }2$ | ? |
| $90:\ \text{power of }3$ | ? |
| $90:\ \text{power of }5$ | ? |

<details>
<summary><b>Working</b></summary>

**1 · Break up 84.**

$$
\begin{aligned}
84&=2\times42 \\
&=2\times2\times21 \\
&=2\times2\times3\times7
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
84=\boxed{2^2\times3\times7}
$$

**2 · Break up 126.**

$$
\begin{aligned}
126&=2\times63 \\
&=2\times3\times21 \\
&=2\times3\times3\times7
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
126=\boxed{2\times3^2\times7}
$$

**3 · Compare the prime bases.**

The primes appearing in both are **2, 3 and 7**.

A prime factor is the prime itself; its power records how often it occurs.

**Practice check — after you try:**

$$
60=\boxed{2^2\times3\times5}
$$

$$
90=\boxed{2\times3^2\times5}
$$

Common prime factors: **2, 3 and 5**.

</details>

## 2. Style 2 · HCF and LCM from primes

**How it works — extension**

- **HCF:** take only primes common to both numbers, with the **smaller power** of each.
- **LCM:** take every prime present, with the **larger power** of each.
- For two positive whole numbers, HCF times LCM equals their product.

- **Worked example:** factorise **72 and 120**.
- Find their HCF and LCM, then check the product rule.

- **Your turn:** Factorise **48 and 150**.
- Use the prime factors to find the HCF and LCM, then check their product against the product of the original numbers.

| Question | Answer |
|---:|---:|
| $\operatorname{HCF}(48,150)$ | ? |
| $\operatorname{LCM}(48,150)$ | ? |
| $\text{HCF}\times\text{LCM}$ | ? |

<details>
<summary><b>Working</b></summary>

**1 · Write both prime factorisations.**

$$
72=2^3\times3^2
$$

$$
120=2^3\times3\times5
$$

**2 · HCF: the smaller powers of common primes.**

$$
\operatorname{HCF}(72,120)=2^3\times3
$$

$$
\operatorname{HCF}(72,120)=\boxed{24}
$$

Do not include 5: it does not divide 72.

**3 · LCM: the larger powers of every prime.**

$$
\operatorname{LCM}(72,120)=2^3\times3^2\times5
$$

$$
\operatorname{LCM}(72,120)=\boxed{360}
$$

**4 · Check both products.**

$$
24\times360=8640
$$

$$
72\times120=8640
$$

The products agree.

**Practice check — after you try:**

$$
48=2^4\times3
$$

$$
150=2\times3\times5^2
$$

$$
\operatorname{HCF}(48,150)=\boxed{6}
$$

$$
\operatorname{LCM}(48,150)=\boxed{1200}
$$

$$
6\times1200=7200
$$

$$
48\times150=7200
$$

</details>
