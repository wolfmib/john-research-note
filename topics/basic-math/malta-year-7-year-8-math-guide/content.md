# Guide — Malta Year 7 and Year 8 Math

Year 7 and Year 8 follow the same eight units, from number to probability; Year 8 returns to each unit and goes one step further. Labels such as 1-1 and 1-2 name the subtopics on these pages, not official syllabus codes. The outline follows Track 3.

**Year 7 — foundations**

- **Unit 1 Number System:** 1-1 place value and ordering · 1-2 factors, multiples, primes, LCM · 1-3 squares and cubes · 1-4 decimals, percentages, negative numbers
- **Unit 2 Numerical Calculations:** 2-1 BIDMAS · 2-2 rounding · 2-3 fractions · 2-4 percentages of amounts · 2-5 money and direct proportion
- **Unit 3 Algebra:** 3-1 sequences · 3-2 expressions and brackets · 3-3 substitution · 3-4 simple equations · 3-5 coordinates, straight-line graphs, function machines
- **Unit 4 Measurement:** 4-1 angles · 4-2 metric units · 4-3 areas of rectangles, triangles and compound shapes · 4-4 volume of cubes and cuboids · 4-5 time
- **Unit 5 Euclidean Geometry:** 5-1 angles on parallel lines · 5-2 triangles and quadrilaterals · 5-3 nets · 5-4 constructing triangles · 5-5 shapes on coordinates
- **Unit 6 Transformation Geometry:** 6-1 lines of symmetry · 6-2 quadrilaterals by reflection symmetry
- **Unit 7 Statistics:** 7-1 frequency tables · 7-2 bar charts and Carroll diagrams · 7-3 mean, median, mode, range
- **Unit 8 Probability:** 8-1 the language of chance · 8-2 listing outcomes · 8-3 simple probability · 8-4 experimental and theoretical probability

**Year 8 — building on Year 7**

- **Unit 1 Number System:** 1-1 powers · 1-2 LCM of three numbers · 1-3 square and cube roots · 1-4 recurring decimals and fractions
- **Unit 2 Numerical Calculations:** 2-1 prime factorisation · 2-2 negative numbers · 2-3 mixed numbers · 2-4 percentage change · 2-5 currency, ratio, proportion, map scales
- **Unit 3 Algebra:** 3-1 signed terms and brackets · 3-2 substitution and changing the subject · 3-3 unknowns on both sides · 3-4 straight lines $y = mx + c$
- **Unit 4 Measurement:** 4-1 parallelograms and trapezia · 4-2 circumference and area of a circle · 4-3 compound areas · 4-4 surface area of cubes and cuboids
- **Unit 5 Euclidean Geometry:** 5-1 exterior angles · 5-2 quadrilateral problems · 5-3 nets of prisms and pyramids · 5-4 parts of a circle · 5-5 compass constructions
- **Unit 6 Transformation Geometry:** 6-1 reflections · 6-2 rotational symmetry · 6-3 rotations · 6-4 translations by vectors
- **Unit 7 Statistics:** 7-1 pie charts · 7-2 choosing an average · 7-3 outliers and range
- **Unit 8 Probability:** 8-1 probabilities that add up to 1 · 8-2 sample spaces for two events

**Where to start:** Year 7 Units 1–3 (number, calculations, algebra) — every Year 8 unit builds on them.

## Year 7 · Unit 1 — Number System

### 1. LCM of two box sizes

A café sells pastizzi in boxes of 6 and qassatat in boxes of 9. What is the smallest number of each that makes the two totals equal, and how many boxes is that?

<details>
<summary><b>Working</b></summary>

List the multiples of each box size until one number is in both lists:

$$
\begin{aligned} 6&:\ 6,\ 12,\ 18,\ 24,\ \dots \\ 9&:\ 9,\ 18,\ 27,\ \dots \end{aligned}
$$

The first shared multiple is the LCM. 18 is 3 boxes of 6 and 2 boxes of 9:

$$
\text{LCM}(6, 9) = \boxed{18}
$$

</details>

## Year 7 · Unit 2 — Numerical Calculations

### 2. Percentage of an amount

A school trip to Gozo costs €60. A deposit of 35% is paid first. How much is the deposit?

<details>
<summary><b>Working</b></summary>

Build 35% from easy pieces: 10% of 60 is 6, so

$$
30\% = 18, \qquad 5\% = 3
$$

Add the pieces, and check with the fraction:

$$
35\% = 18 + 3 = \tfrac{35}{100}\times 60 = \boxed{21}
$$

The deposit is €21.

</details>

## Year 7 · Unit 3 — Algebra

### 3. Term of a sequence

A hall has 12 chairs in row 1, and each row has 3 more chairs than the row before. How many chairs are in row 10?

<details>
<summary><b>Working</b></summary>

The step is 3, so the rule starts with $3n$. Row 1 has 12 chairs, so add 9:

$$
\text{chairs in row } n = 3n + 9
$$

Put $n = 10$:

$$
3(10) + 9 = \boxed{39}
$$

</details>

## Year 7 · Unit 4 — Measurement

### 4. Area of an L-shape

A balcony is a 6 m by 4 m rectangle with a 2 m by 3 m corner cut out. Find the area of the balcony.

<details>
<summary><b>Working</b></summary>

Area of the whole rectangle and of the cut-out corner:

$$
6\times4 = 24\ \text{m}^2, \qquad 2\times3 = 6\ \text{m}^2
$$

Subtract the corner:

$$
24 - 6 = \boxed{18\ \text{m}^2}
$$

</details>

## Year 7 · Unit 5 — Euclidean Geometry

### 5. Angles on parallel lines

A straight road crosses two parallel streets. At the first street one angle is $65^\circ$. Find the alternate angle and the co-interior angle at the second street.

<details>
<summary><b>Working</b></summary>

Alternate angles (a Z shape) are equal:

$$
\text{alternate} = 65^\circ
$$

Co-interior angles (a C shape) add up to $180^\circ$:

$$
180^\circ - 65^\circ = \boxed{115^\circ}
$$

</details>

## Year 7 · Unit 6 — Transformation Geometry

### 6. Symmetry of quadrilaterals

How many lines of symmetry do a rectangle, a rhombus, a kite and a parallelogram have?

<details>
<summary><b>Working</b></summary>

A rectangle folds onto itself along the two lines through midpoints of opposite sides; a rhombus along its two diagonals; a kite along one diagonal only; a parallelogram (not a rectangle or rhombus) never.

$$
\text{rectangle, rhombus, kite, parallelogram:}\ \boxed{2,\ 2,\ 1,\ 0}
$$

</details>

## Year 7 · Unit 7 — Statistics

### 7. Mean, median, mode and range

A team scores these goals in 8 matches: 1, 4, 2, 0, 2, 6, 2, 3. Find the mean, median, mode and range.

<details>
<summary><b>Working</b></summary>

Order the data and add it up:

$$
0,\ 1,\ 2,\ 2,\ 2,\ 3,\ 4,\ 6 \qquad \text{total} = 20
$$

Mean = total ÷ count; the median is halfway between the 4th and 5th values; 2 appears most often; range = largest − smallest:

$$
\boxed{\text{mean } 2.5,\ \text{median } 2,\ \text{mode } 2,\ \text{range } 6}
$$

</details>

## Year 7 · Unit 8 — Probability

### 8. Simple probability

A bag holds 3 red, 5 blue and 2 green counters. One counter is taken at random. Find P(blue) and P(not green).

<details>
<summary><b>Working</b></summary>

There are $3 + 5 + 2 = 10$ counters, all equally likely:

$$
P(\text{blue}) = \frac{5}{10} = \boxed{\frac12}
$$

Not green means red or blue, $3 + 5 = 8$ counters:

$$
P(\text{not green}) = \frac{8}{10} = \boxed{\frac45}
$$

</details>

## Year 8 · Unit 1 — Number System

### 9. Terminating or recurring

Does $\frac{7}{40}$ give a terminating or a recurring decimal? Find the decimal.

<details>
<summary><b>Working</b></summary>

The fraction is in simplest form, and the denominator has only 2s and 5s, so the decimal terminates:

$$
40 = 2^3\times5
$$

Divide 7 by 40:

$$
\frac{7}{40} = \boxed{0.175}
$$

</details>

## Year 8 · Unit 2 — Numerical Calculations

### 10. Percentage increase

A season ticket costs €80. Next year the price rises by 15%. What is the new price?

<details>
<summary><b>Working</b></summary>

An increase of 15% keeps 100% and adds 15%, so multiply by 1.15:

$$
100\% + 15\% = 115\% = 1.15
$$

Apply the multiplier:

$$
80\times1.15 = \boxed{92}
$$

The new price is €92.

</details>

## Year 8 · Unit 3 — Algebra

### 11. Unknowns on both sides

Solve $3(x + 4) = x + 20$.

<details>
<summary><b>Working</b></summary>

Expand the bracket:

$$
3x + 12 = x + 20
$$

Take $x$ and 12 from both sides, then divide by 2:

$$
2x = 8 \quad\Rightarrow\quad \boxed{x = 4}
$$

Check: $3(4 + 4) = 24$ and $4 + 20 = 24$.

</details>

## Year 8 · Unit 4 — Measurement

### 12. Area of a trapezium

A garden is a trapezium with parallel sides of 8 m and 5 m, 4 m apart. Find its area.

<details>
<summary><b>Working</b></summary>

Area of a trapezium = half the sum of the parallel sides × the distance between them:

$$
A = \tfrac12(a + b)h
$$

Substitute:

$$
A = \tfrac12(8 + 5)\times4 = \boxed{26\ \text{m}^2}
$$

</details>

## Year 8 · Unit 5 — Euclidean Geometry

### 13. Exterior angle of a triangle

In triangle ABC, angle A is $48^\circ$ and angle B is $67^\circ$. Side BC is extended beyond C. Find the exterior angle at C.

<details>
<summary><b>Working</b></summary>

An exterior angle equals the sum of the two interior angles opposite it:

$$
48^\circ + 67^\circ = \boxed{115^\circ}
$$

Check: the interior angle at C is $180^\circ - 115^\circ = 65^\circ$, and $48^\circ + 67^\circ + 65^\circ = 180^\circ$.

</details>

## Year 8 · Unit 6 — Transformation Geometry

### 14. Rotation about the origin

Rotate the point $(4, 1)$ through $90^\circ$ anticlockwise about the origin.

<details>
<summary><b>Working</b></summary>

A quarter turn anticlockwise sends $(x, y)$ to $(-y, x)$:

$$
(4, 1) \to \boxed{(-1, 4)}
$$

</details>

## Year 8 · Unit 7 — Statistics

### 15. Pie chart angle

In a survey of 36 students, 15 walk to school. Find the angle of the walking sector in a pie chart.

<details>
<summary><b>Working</b></summary>

The sector takes the same fraction of $360^\circ$ as the group takes of the class:

$$
\frac{15}{36}\times360^\circ = \boxed{150^\circ}
$$

</details>

## Year 8 · Unit 8 — Probability

### 16. Two events together

A fair die is rolled and a fair coin is tossed. Find the probability of an even number and heads.

<details>
<summary><b>Working</b></summary>

The sample space has $6\times2 = 12$ equally likely outcomes. The favourable ones are 2H, 4H and 6H:

$$
P = \frac{3}{12} = \boxed{\frac14}
$$

</details>
