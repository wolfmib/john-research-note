# Malta Year 8 Math — Squares, Cubes & Roots

- Squaring means multiplying a number by itself.
- Cubing means multiplying three copies of a number.
- Square roots undo squares; cube roots undo cubes.
- Work without a calculator and recognise perfect squares and cubes.

## 1. Style 1 · Squares and square roots

**How it works**

- A square uses two copies of the number, not multiplication by 2.
- The square-root symbol asks for the **non-negative** number whose square gives the value.
- Calculate each square or root before adding.

**Worked example:** calculate without a calculator.

- **(a)** $13^2$
- **(b)** $\sqrt{225}$
- **(c)** $7^2+9^2$
- **(d)** $\sqrt{144}+\sqrt{81}$

- **Your turn:** Calculate each part without a calculator.
- Show your working on paper.

| Question | Answer |
|---:|---:|
| $12^2$ | ? |
| $\sqrt{196}$ | ? |
| $8^2+6^2$ | ? |
| $\sqrt{169}+\sqrt{64}$ | ? |

<details>
<summary><b>Working</b></summary>

**(a) Multiply two copies.**

$$
\begin{aligned}
13^2&=13\times13 \\
&=\boxed{169}
\end{aligned}
$$

**(b) Find the number that squares to 225.**

$$
15\times15=225
$$

$$
\sqrt{225}=\boxed{15}
$$

**(c) Square each number first.**

$$
\begin{aligned}
7^2+9^2&=49+81 \\
&=\boxed{130}
\end{aligned}
$$

**(d) Find each root first.**

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{144}+\sqrt{81}&=12+9 \\
&=\boxed{21}
\end{aligned}
$$

**Practice check — after you try:**

$$
12^2=\boxed{144}
$$

$$
\sqrt{196}=\boxed{14}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
8^2+6^2&=64+36 \\
&=\boxed{100}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{169}+\sqrt{64}&=13+8 \\
&=\boxed{21}
\end{aligned}
$$

</details>

## 2. Style 2 · Cubes and cube roots

**How it works**

- If a cube has the same number of unit cubes along each edge, multiply that number three times to get the total.
- To find the edge count from the total, take the **cube root**.

**Worked example:** a large cube contains **216 unit cubes**.

- Find the number along each edge and evaluate $\sqrt[3]{216}$.
- Repeat for a cube containing **343 unit cubes**.
- Explain why cube roots are useful here.

- **Your turn:** A cube contains **125 unit cubes**.
- How many are along each edge?
- Repeat for a cube containing **512 unit cubes**.
- Explain why you used a cube root.

| Question | Answer |
|---:|---:|
| $\sqrt[3]{125}$ | ? |
| $\sqrt[3]{512}$ | ? |

<details>
<summary><b>Working</b></summary>

**1 · Think of three equal dimensions.**

The number along an edge is multiplied by itself three times to count all the unit cubes.

**2 · Find three equal factors of 216.**

$$
6\times6\times6=216
$$

$$
\sqrt[3]{216}=\boxed{6}
$$

There are **6 unit cubes along each edge**.

**3 · Repeat for 343.**

$$
7\times7\times7=343
$$

$$
\sqrt[3]{343}=\boxed{7}
$$

There are **7 unit cubes along each edge**.

**4 · Why a cube root?** It reverses cubing: the total counts all three dimensions; the cube root recovers one edge.

**Practice check — after you try:**

$$
5^3=125
$$

$$
\sqrt[3]{125}=\boxed{5}
$$

$$
8^3=512
$$

$$
\sqrt[3]{512}=\boxed{8}
$$

</details>
