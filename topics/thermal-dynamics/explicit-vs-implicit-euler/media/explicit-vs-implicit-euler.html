Therma thermal dynamics · Explicit vs implicit Euler on a falling ball

The physics model

A ball falling with air resistance
$y(t)$ = height,   $v(t)$ = velocity (positive upward)
$g = 9.81\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$,   $\beta = 0.1\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}$ (air resistance)
$$\frac{dv}{dt} = -g - \beta v \qquad \frac{dy}{dt} = v$$
• Gravity pulls the ball downward: $-g$.
• Air resistance opposes the motion: $-\beta v$.
• Initial state: $y(0) = 100\ \mathrm{m}$, $v(0) = 0$.
This is the physical model. Its exact solution is the reference.

The exact solution

The differential equation solved analytically
$$v(t) = -\frac{g}{\beta}\left(1 - e^{-\beta t}\right)$$ $$y(t) = 100 - \frac{g}{\beta}\left[t - \frac{1 - e^{-\beta t}}{\beta}\right]$$
The ball speeds up, and air resistance slows the gain in speed.
At $t = 4\ \mathrm{s}$ the exact height is $y = 31.016\ \mathrm{m}$. This curve is the benchmark for the two numerical methods.

Deriving explicit Euler

The derivative is evaluated with the known values at step $n$
$$\frac{v^{n+1} - v^n}{\Delta t} = -g - \beta\, v^{n}$$
Rearranged for $v^{n+1}$:
$$v^{n+1} = v^n - \Delta t\,(g + \beta\, v^n)$$
And for the position:
$$y^{n+1} = y^n + \Delta t\, v^{n}$$
Only known values at step $n$ appear on the right side. Each step is a direct computation.

Explicit Euler in action

Large time step: $\Delta t = 0.8\ \mathrm{s}$
The explicit ball jumps once per step. Each jump uses the velocity at the start of the step.
The ball speeds up while it falls, so the older, slower velocity moves it too little.
Explicit Euler stays above the exact height.

Deriving implicit Euler

The derivative is evaluated with the unknown values at step $n+1$
$$\frac{v^{n+1} - v^n}{\Delta t} = -g - \beta\, v^{n+1}$$
$v^{n+1}$ appears on both sides. Solve for it:
$$v^{n+1} + \beta\Delta t\, v^{n+1} = v^n - g\,\Delta t \qquad\Rightarrow\qquad v^{n+1} = \frac{v^n - g\,\Delta t}{1 + \beta\Delta t}$$
And for the position:
$$y^{n+1} = y^n + \Delta t\, v^{n+1}$$
Each step is an equation for the new value. Here it is solved by hand; for a large system it is a linear solve.

Implicit Euler in action

Large time step: $\Delta t = 0.8\ \mathrm{s}$
The implicit ball jumps once per step. Each jump uses the velocity at the end of the step.
The newer, faster velocity is applied to the whole step, so the ball moves too much.
Implicit Euler stays below the exact height.

Curves together

Time step $\Delta t = 0.8\ \mathrm{s}$, then smaller
Height against time. The exact curve is continuous; the two methods give one point per step.
With $\Delta t = 0.8\ \mathrm{s}$ both methods deviate from the exact height. Explicit is too high; implicit is too low.
As $\Delta t$ shrinks, both curves move onto the exact curve.

How does $\Delta t$ affect the accuracy?

Absolute height error at $t = 4\ \mathrm{s}$ (exact height $31.0160\ \mathrm{m}$)
1. Both explicit and implicit Euler are numerical approximations.
2. The time step $\Delta t$ sets the accuracy.
3. As $\Delta t$ becomes smaller, both methods converge to the exact height.
Smaller $\Delta t$ gives better agreement with the exact solution.
