Therma thermal dynamics · Pennes bioheat equation
$\rho c\,\dfrac{\partial T}{\partial t}$$=$$\nabla\cdot(k\,\nabla T)$$+$$\omega_b\,\rho_b c_b\,(T_b - T)$$+$$Q_m$
Energy balance for one tissue volume cell.
Each term is a thermal power per unit volume.
Rate of stored thermal energy change
$\rho$: $\mathrm{kg/m^3}$
$c$: $\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$
$\partial T/\partial t$: $\mathrm{K/s}$
Result: $\mathrm{J/(m^3\cdot s)} = \mathrm{W/m^3}$
The left term is energy storage. It tells how fast the tissue temperature changes.
Net heat exchange with neighboring tissue
$k$: $\mathrm{W/(m\cdot K)}$
$\nabla T$: $\mathrm{K/m}$
$k\,\nabla T$: $\mathrm{W/m^2}$
Result: $\nabla\cdot(k\,\nabla T)$: $\mathrm{W/m^3}$
This term is heat conduction. It describes heat exchange with neighboring tissue.
Heat exchange between blood and tissue
$\omega_b$: $\mathrm{1/s}$
$\rho_b$: $\mathrm{kg/m^3}$
$c_b$: $\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$
$(T_b - T)$: $\mathrm{K}$
Result: $\mathrm{J/(m^3\cdot s)} = \mathrm{W/m^3}$
This term is blood perfusion. Blood can bring heat to the tissue or remove heat from it.
Internal heat generation by metabolism
$Q_m$: $\mathrm{W/m^3}$
This term is metabolic heat. The tissue generates heat inside itself.
All terms have unit $\mathrm{W/m^3}$
Stored energy change =
Conduction + Blood perfusion + Metabolism
The Pennes equation is a thermal power balance per unit volume.
