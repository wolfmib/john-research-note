A Recovery Curve as a Vector

A curve sampled at $K$ time points is one vector of $K$ values.
→
$$f = \begin{bmatrix} f(t_1) \\ f(t_2) \\ \vdots \\ f(t_K) \end{bmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^K$$
$f_m$  model curve: the double exponential with one class's averaged fitted parameters, scaled so that $\|f_m\| = 1$
$f_n$  one modeled curve from the lesion set or the non-lesion set of a case
Three measures compare $f_n$ with $f_m$: projection, correlation, distance.

1. Projection

The inner product of the curve $f_n$ with the model curve $f_m$.
$$\mathrm{proj}_n = \langle \textcolor{#0d6efd}{f_n},\, \textcolor{#c77700}{f_m} \rangle = \textcolor{#0d6efd}{f_n}^{\mathsf T}\, \textcolor{#c77700}{f_m}$$
$$= \textcolor{#0d6efd}{f_n(t_1)}\,\textcolor{#c77700}{f_m(t_1)} + \textcolor{#0d6efd}{f_n(t_2)}\,\textcolor{#c77700}{f_m(t_2)} + \cdots + \textcolor{#0d6efd}{f_n(t_K)}\,\textcolor{#c77700}{f_m(t_K)}$$
sample by sample: multiply the two values at the same time point, then add
$\|\textcolor{#c77700}{f_m}\| = 1$  →  $\mathrm{proj}_n$ is the length of $\textcolor{#0d6efd}{f_n}$ along $\textcolor{#c77700}{f_m}$
How much of the curve lies along the model curve.

2. Correlation

Do the two curves move above and below their own means at the same time points?
$$\textcolor{#c77700}{a_k = f_m(t_k) - \bar{f}_m} \qquad \textcolor{#0d6efd}{b_k = f_n(t_k) - \bar{f}_n}$$
deviation of each sample from its own curve's mean (the vertical bars)
$\rho = \dfrac{\mathrm{cov}(f_m, f_n)}{\sigma_{f_m}\,\sigma_{f_n}}$
$= \dfrac{\textcolor{#198754}{\sum_k a_k\, b_k}}{\textcolor{#c77700}{\sqrt{\sum_k a_k^2}}\;\textcolor{#0d6efd}{\sqrt{\sum_k b_k^2}}}$
numerator: the sum of the green bars  ·  denominator: the size of each curve's own deviations
These two curves:  $\rho$ =
Both curves are below their means early and above them late, so every product is positive and $\rho$ is close to 1. A sample where one curve is above its mean and the other is below gives a negative product and lowers $\rho$.

3. Distance

The Euclidean norm of the difference between the two curves.
$$d_n = \frac{1}{K-1}\, \textcolor{#198754}{\| f_n - f_m \|}$$
$$\textcolor{#198754}{\| f_n - f_m \|} = \sqrt{\sum_{k=1}^{K} \big(\textcolor{#0d6efd}{f_n(t_k)} - \textcolor{#c77700}{f_m(t_k)}\big)^2}$$
sample by sample: the gap between the two values, squared, then added
The straight-line gap between the two vectors, divided by $K-1$.

Three Measures per Curve

Each compares one curve $f_n$ with the model curve $f_m$.

1. Projection

$\mathrm{proj}_n = \langle f_n, f_m \rangle$

2. Correlation

$\rho = \dfrac{\mathrm{cov}(f_m, f_n)}{\sigma_{f_m}\,\sigma_{f_n}}$

3. Distance

$d_n = \dfrac{1}{K-1}\,\| f_n - f_m \|$
Each measure is computed for a set of at least 10 curves $f_n$; its mean and standard deviation are the features. 2 model curves (benign, malignant) × 2 sets (lesion, non-lesion) × 6 values = 24 features.
