Model P (linear): $\hat y = 1.36 + 6.39x$
$\text{residual} = \text{actual} - \text{predicted}$
$r = y - \hat y$
What the sign of a residual says
The model underestimates
$x = 0$ and $x = 5$: the actual value is above the line.
$r > 0$: the model predicted too little.
The model overestimates
$x = 1, 2, 3, 4$: the actual value is below the line.
$r < 0$: the model predicted too much.
The residuals go $+,\ -,\ -,\ -,\ -,\ +$: a U-shape.
A pattern means a line is the wrong model type.
Model Q: $\hat y = 5(1.5)^x$
Same data, same residual $r = y - \hat y$
Every residual is between $-0.2$ and $0.2$.
The signs switch above and below $0$ with no pattern.
A U-shape:
a straight line is the wrong model type.
Small, with no pattern:
the exponential model fits.
A good model leaves small residuals with no pattern.
